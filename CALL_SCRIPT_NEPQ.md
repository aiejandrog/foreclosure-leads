# BSG Call Mode — Refined Script (NEPQ Black Book integration)

**Source:** Jeremy Miner, *NEPQ Black Book of Questions* (7th Level, 2024), mined in full 2026-09-13.
**Adapted to:** Biscayne Solutions Group foreclosure-workout calls — distressed Florida homeowners,
2–4 weeks from a sale date, who have already been called by a dozen "investors."
**This is the source copy** for `call_mode.py`. Jesse/Alejandro veto copy; approved lines get wired into the page.

---

## Non-negotiable guardrails (unchanged — every line below obeys these)

- **"Options," never a promised outcome or date.** Never "we'll save your home."
- **We INTRODUCE a licensed senior advisor.** Never "place loans," never a rate as ours.
- **Money side only.** Never the attorney's case or its merits.
- **"Our senior advisor, 30+ years."** Never "my supervisor / our attorneys / my clients."
- **No money up front, ever** (MARS + FS 501.1377). The consult is free.
- **We are not buying the house.** Say it in the opener.
- **No either/or that hands them an exit** (Jesse's rule). The only either/or allowed is two ways to say *yes* — a time, never a whether.
- **All-party recording consent** (FL 934.03) if the line records — on screen, read out loud.
- **Spanish is *usted*, always.** Never *tú*.
- **Calls end warm.** Never argue.

---

## The shape of an NEPQ call (why the order matters)

Miner's whole thesis: **the homeowner talks themselves into the problem — you never push.** 85% of the
work is the questions (Engagement); the "pitch" (booking the advisor) is 10–15% and should feel like
the obvious next step. You get there by *tone* and *questions*, not by talking.

```
DISARM  →  STATUS FRAME  →  SITUATION  →  PROBLEM AWARENESS  →  (PROBING LOOP)
        →  SOLUTION AWARENESS  →  CONSEQUENCE  →  TRANSITION/BRIDGE  →  BOOK THE ADVISOR
```

**Tonality is load-bearing** (Miner is emphatic). The `…` in every line = a real pause. Slow down.
- **Curious/confused** in the opener (lowers your status, raises their guard-down).
- **Concerned** through Problem Awareness and Solution Awareness.
- **Challenging** on Consequence (you're pushing them to defend why they need to change).
- **Neutral/warm** on the close.

## STAGE 0 — RECORDING CONSENT (only if the line records)

> "Quick thing before we start — I record my calls so I keep your file straight. That okay with you?"

A **no** means *stop recording*, not hang up.

---

## STAGE 1 — THE OPENER (disarm, ~15 seconds, then STOP)

Pick the variant off the lead card. Keep it to name → two "nots" → the reason → a tiny ask.

**Cold (name known):**
> "Hi, is this {first}?… It's {sender} with Biscayne Solutions Group. This might sound a little
> random… but I've got a copy of the court paperwork on {st1} right in front of me… and I was
> hoping you could help me out for a second. I'm **not** your lender, and I'm **not** calling to
> buy the place. Can I ask you something real quick?"

**Anon (no name / not confirmed owner):**
> "Hi, am I speaking with the owner of {st1}?…" + same body.

**Warm (prior Talked/Callback/Appt on this person):**
> "{first}!… it's {sender}, from Biscayne Solutions Group. How've you been?… Listen, I was looking
> at your file on {st1} again and something jumped out I wanted to run by you. You got a quick second?"

> **NEPQ note:** "just" and "real quick" imply low stakes and that they should already know you.
> The two "nots" kill the two objections a distressed owner is already loaded with (*you're the bank
> / you want my house*). This is the Black Book's cold-call intro ("I was wondering if you could help
> me out for a moment?") adapted — lower status on purpose.

---

## STAGE 2 — THE STATUS FRAME  ⭐ NEW (the biggest missing piece)

Right after they say "…okay, what is it?" — before any question. This is the move that separates NEPQ
from every other caller they've hung up on. Say it *neutral, expert, unhurried:*

> "Honestly, this is pretty basic — I'm really just trying to understand where things stand on {st1}
> right now… compared to where you'd actually want them to land… so we can see what that gap looks
> like. And toward the end, if you feel like it *might* be worth it, we can talk about a couple of
> **possible** next steps. Would that help you?"

> **Why it works:** it positions *you* as the calm authority (not the needy salesperson), it removes
> the "he's going to pitch me" pressure, and their "yes… that'd help" is the first micro-commitment.
> Everything after is built on it.

**ES:**
> "La verdad, esto es bien sencillo — solo quiero entender cómo está la situación con {st1} ahora
> mismo… comparado con dónde a usted le gustaría que quedara… para ver cómo se ve esa diferencia. Y
> al final, si usted siente que *pudiera* valer la pena, hablamos de un par de **posibles** pasos a
> seguir. ¿Le parece que eso le ayudaría?"

---

## STAGE 3 — SITUATION (get the facts, in their words)

One open question. Never a binary. Their answer picks which program the advisor even pitches.

> "Before I get into any of it… what is it you're actually hoping happens with {st1}?"

Follow-ups as needed (fact-finding, curious tone):
- "How long has this been going on?"
- "What got you to this point, if you don't mind me asking?"
- "Who've you talked to about it so far — the bank, an attorney, anybody?"

---

## STAGE 4 — PROBLEM AWARENESS (build the gap; they say the pain, not you)

> "When the bank or the attorney talked to you about that sale date… did anybody actually explain
> what happens to your balance every time that date gets pushed back?"

Almost always: *"No, nobody really explained it."* **Let the silence sit.** Then the money fact, handed
straight back as a question:

> "That's what I hear from just about everybody. Every time it moves… the interest and the legal fees
> keep stacking on top of what you already owe. Have you had a chance to see where that number's really
> sitting today… or is it kind of a moving target?"

### The Probing Loop  ⭐ NEW (drop these in whenever they get vague)

This is Miner's actual engine — mirror their last words back as a question, get specific:
- "Not 100%?…" *(echo whatever they said)*
- "What do you mean by that?"
- "Can I ask what you meant when you said…?"
- "How long's *that* been going on?"
- "Has that had an impact on you?" → "In what way, though?"
- "You don't sound so sure… what is it you don't like about how it's going?"
- "Can you give me a specific example of when that happened?"

### Two Truths (if they insist everything's handled)

> "So to me it sounds like this is basically 100% under control for you. Is there *anything* you'd
> change about how it's being handled… if you could?"

Most people, hearing "100%," will crack it open: *"Well, not 100%…"* → **"Not 100%?…"** → listen.

---

## STAGE 5 — SOLUTION AWARENESS (what they've tried + what "solved" looks like)

**Part 1 — what they've already done** (surfaces the plan they have; you *insure* it, never fight it):

> "Before today… were you out there looking at options so this didn't go to sale… or what were you
> doing about it?"
- If *no*: "I'm curious — what's held you back from doing that so far?"
- If *yes*: "What do you feel held that back from actually working?" / "Can I ask why you were
  considering that as the option?"

**Part 2 — the objective state** (get them to feel the future — this is the beat the old stack was thin on):

> "Let's say the sale gets postponed and you actually get room to breathe on {st1}… what would that
> let you do that you can't right now?"
> "How would that be different… than how things feel today?"
> "What would that mean for you and your family… personally?"

> **NEPQ note:** if the advisor can save them money, **downplay it** ("it's probably not huge, but…").
> Humans inflate a number you shrink. That's *mismatching*.

---

## STAGE 6 — CONSEQUENCE (rip the future away; they defend why they must act now)

Challenging/concerned tone. Take a problem *they* named and ask the consequence around it.

> "Let me ask you straight — if nothing changes… and that date comes and goes… where does that leave
> you and your family?"

Then the tougher rail (⭐ NEW — pick one, don't stack):
- "Do you want to have to go through all that… if you… if you didn't have to?"
- "Can you afford to take that risk?" *(lean in, empathy, not a threat)*
- "Are you willing to just settle for that, though?… Well — whose choice is it, if you settle or not?"
- "Why look at fixing this now?… Why not just push it down the road like a lot of people do… who end
  up losing the house they could've kept?"

Land it on an assumed statement + tie-down (never a question that lets them not care):
> "That's not something you're willing to just sit back and let happen. **Right?**"

---

## STAGE 7 — TRANSITION / BRIDGE  ⭐ NEW (then hand off to the advisor)

Miner's transition formula, in their words + their emotion:

> "Okay… based on everything you just told me… this is exactly the kind of thing we can actually help
> with. Because you said {repeat what they want}… and the way that date keeps moving has you feeling
> {repeat their emotion — stressed / worried / stuck}… I think you mentioned that a couple times."

Then the advisor ask + fairness close (the only either/or = two ways to say yes):
> "So here's the one thing I'd want for you before that date hits: our senior advisor — 30-plus years
> in mortgages and foreclosure workouts — takes about five minutes and lays your real options out.
> Worst case, you know more than you did this morning. That's fair, right?… Let me get you on his
> calendar. Is later today better… or first thing tomorrow?"

## OBJECTIONS — the NEPQ way (question, don't argue; CIOC skeleton kept)

**Framework stays CIOC** (Cushion → Isolate → Overcome → Close). The Black Book sharpens each one:

### "I'm not interested" (the reflex brush-off) — NEW, now in the objection picker (card 15)
It's a reflex, not a decision — said before they know what the call is. Don't answer the "no"; disarm
it and reopen with a curious question. Never push back (it sets harder).
> "That's not a problem at all — honestly, I wasn't expecting you to be. Almost everybody I reach
> about a place like yours says the exact same thing when I first call… I'm not signing you up for
> anything, and I'm not calling to buy the house. Real quick though, so I'm not the twelfth person
> wasting your afternoon — has anybody actually shown you what happens to your balance every time
> that sale date gets pushed… or is that still kind of a question mark?"
>
> *(then)* "Because that's the only reason I called — not to pitch you a loan. Our senior advisor,
> 30-plus years, lays your real options out in five minutes, free. Worst case you know more than you
> did this morning, and we part friends. That's fair, right?"

**One sentence:** *"I'm not asking you to be interested — I'm asking if it's worth five free minutes
to know your options before that date hits."*

### "Just send me something in writing / mail me the info"
*(the foreclosure-call version of Miner's "send me a proposal/email")*
> "Yeah, I can do that for sure… now, just so I send you the *right* thing and not a stack of junk —
> what specifically would you want it to show you?"

Whatever they say **is their real concern.** Then pivot into a Situation question, and at the end:
> "So let's say you look it over and it lines up with what you told me… what would you want the next
> step to be?" — **never** send blind; that's how you never hear back.

### "I'm busy / call me back later"  →  NEPQ Calendar Commitment ⭐ NEW
> "No problem at all… what I'll do — I'll give you my number, and you'd have to call *me* back later
> to catch me… what's your timeframe on getting back to me, just to see if I'd even be free?"

Or, if they'll give a day:
> "It might be tough to catch me cold with my schedule… if you've got your calendar handy I'll pull
> mine and we'll lock a specific time so neither of us is chasing the other — that work?"

*(Flips status: your time is the scarce thing. If they no-show the callback, wait 2 minutes, then call:
"Hey {first}, it's just {sender} getting back to you — running a little behind…")*

### "I already have a plan / a lawyer / a modification going"
**Insure it — you're the parachute, not the enemy** (CIOC OVERCOME, kept):
> "Good — keep it. I don't want to be your bank or your middleman… I want to be your parachute. You're
> betting the house on that plan landing on time. Keep it exactly as-is… and let our advisor look at
> postponing the sale in parallel, for free. If your plan lands first, I did you a favor and you owe
> me a coffee. If it slips a day — and here a day is everything — you've already got backup. Fair?"

If they're still flat (skeptical tone, Miner's reactivation):
> "Can I ask you something?… How can I point out that you *might* be missing something here… without
> you getting upset with me?"

### "How do I know you're legit / send references"
99% of the time this is a concern in disguise:
> "Totally fair — what specifically would you want to be sure of, so I point you to the right thing?"
Answer *that*, then re-ask for the five minutes.

### Price / "what's this cost"
> "The records review with the advisor is free — you're never asked to pay us anything up front. What
> matters more to you: what it costs… or actually keeping the room to save the house?" *(results-based
> reframe; never quote a fee)*

### The 15-second bail-out (when they're clearly done)
> "Totally understand — one thing and I'm gone: our senior advisor, 30-plus years, reviews your case
> free, five minutes, right on the phone. If nothing fits, we part friends. Fair?"

## CLOSE — measure what they DID, not what they said (kept)

- Fairness micro-agreements throughout: *"That's fair, right?"* / *"Make sense?"*
- Paperwork trial close: *"Let me get the paperwork together — if we agree we shake hands; if not,
  I wasted fifteen minutes and we part friends."*
- Read-back test: *"Save my number right now… now read it all back to me."* Clean read-back = real.
  "Pen ran out of ink" = don't count the deal.

## NEVER SAY

- Any promised outcome or date. "Options," never "we'll save your home."
- Anything about the attorney's case or its merits. Money side only.
- "We place loans," or any rate as ours. Licensed lenders lend; we introduce.
- "My supervisor" / "our attorneys" / "my clients" / company + "30 years."
- Money up front. No fees, ever.
- "Foreclosure" to someone who isn't the owner.
- An argument. Calls end warm.
